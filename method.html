The Method

The principles behind every session, written down. To be practised, shared and, in time, handed on.

The thesis

The environment changes.
The principles remain.

Mystique Tao meets you where you are. Each experience is shaped by the moment, the space and the people present.

The space.
A meeting room. A terrace. Open ground. Whatever is there, used as it is.

Seven principles

What every session is built from.

Six elements, and a seventh that holds them. Each has three qualities, a meaning, and a place in the arc.

  1. Movement

    Strength. Mobility. Function.

    In practice

    Movement wakes the body and gives back its range. Strength to hold. Mobility to reach. Function to carry it into the day.

    In a session

    It opens the arc. Standing, on the floor or seated, scaled to the bodies present. Function before form.

  2. Breath

    Rhythm. Regulation. Energy.

    In practice

    The one rhythm that runs by itself and can still be guided. Slower out than in, and the body follows. Regulation first, energy after.

    In a session

    The thread between the parts. Movement slows into breath. Breath lowers into rest. Breath brings everyone back.

  3. Awareness

    Presence. Focus. Clarity.

    In practice

    Attention, placed. On the breath, the ground, the room. Clarity is what is left when the noise settles.

    In a session

    Never a separate exercise. It runs underneath everything: the body noticed as it moves, the breath as it changes, the moment as it is.

  4. Deep Rest

    Yoga Nidra. Relaxation. Recovery.

    In practice

    Yoga Nidra: lying down, guided by voice, awake. The body lets go while awareness stays. Relaxation becomes recovery.

    In a session

    It follows effort, never replaces it. Bodies down, eyes closed, warm and still, for as long as the setting allows.

  5. Sound

    Vibration. Resonance. Balance.

    In practice

    Vibration felt before it is heard. Bowls struck by hand, the voice, the silence after. The room comes into one rhythm.

    In a session

    It marks the thresholds: arriving, settling, returning. While everyone rests, it holds the space.

  6. Nature

    Connection. Grounding. Renewal.

    In practice

    A participant, not a backdrop. Ground under bare feet, open air, weather as it is. Something remembered rather than learned.

    In a session

    Outside whenever the sky allows. Indoors it still arrives: an open window, daylight, the floor felt as ground.

  7. Experience

    Embodiment. Integration. Transformation.

    In practice

    Not a technique. What the six become when they are lived together, in one body, in one arc.

    In a session

    It is how every session closes: a pause to let it land before returning to the day. Nothing to add. Something to notice.

Three dualities

The balancing rules.

Opposites are held, not resolved. Each pair is a dial, set for the room. One for tone. One for rhythm. One for intensity.

  • Tone

    Seriousness & Playfulness

    Guide with precision. Hold it lightly.

    Care in every cue, and room to laugh when a balance wobbles. Too serious, and the body braces. Too playful, and attention scatters.

  • Rhythm

    Stillness & Movement

    Move before stillness. Return to stillness after moving.

    A body that has moved can settle. A body that has been still wants to move. The session swings between the two until both feel natural.

  • Intensity

    Effort & Release

    Ask for effort. Then let it go completely.

    Release is clearest straight after effort. Neither one is the goal. The passage between them is.

The person

Seven layers. One whole.

The method works with a whole person, not a part. A session moves through these layers in order, from the most tangible to the widest, and ends on the one that holds the rest.

Each layer has a principle that reaches it.

  1. Body. through movement
  2. Breath. through breath
  3. Mind. through awareness
  4. Heart. through deep rest & sound
  5. Connection. through the people present
  6. Nature. through nature
  7. Whole. through experience

A session

How a session is composed.

The tagline is the arc: move, breathe, rest, experience. Add an arrival, and it becomes a session. It rises into effort and settles into release.

Effort Release

  1. Arrive.

    Awareness

    Settle into the space. Notice the ground, the air, the people present. Nothing to achieve yet.

  2. Move.

    Movement

    Wake the body. Range, warmth, a little effort.

  3. Breathe.

    Breath

    Slow the rhythm. Longer out than in, until the body follows.

  4. Rest.

    Deep Rest · Sound

    Lie down and let go. Deep rest, guided by voice, held by sound.

  5. Integrate.

    Experience

    Return slowly. Notice what changed. Carry it back into the day.

Awareness and nature are not steps. They run underneath the whole arc, as far as the setting allows.

Six settings. One method.

The principles stay. Their weight shifts. A workplace leans on breath, awareness and rest that fit a working day. A retreat has room for all seven, in depth. In nature, the setting leads.

Passing it on

Held now. Handed on in time.

A method lives in people before it lives on paper. What follows are intentions, stated openly. None of it is a programme yet.

  • Recordings.

    Practices to return to, daily, for the parts that survive a recording: breath, awareness, deep rest. A few short ones are already shared on YouTube (opens in a new tab).

    What needs presence stays in person: the room held together, the ground underfoot.

  • A written method.

    This page is its first draft. The intention is a short text, kept and revised as practice teaches more, so the principles can be read as well as felt.

  • Future facilitators.

    In time, others who can hold the same principles in their own environments, in their own voice. Not a training yet. An intention, so the method is built to be handed on.
